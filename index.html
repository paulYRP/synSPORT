Connect the decisions

Generation is part
of a wider process.

Preparing, generating and assessing synthetic data are connected activities. The sport framework organises these decisions around the intended user and operating context.

The comparison brings established synthesis guidance into conversation with six dimensions for sport.

Make the purpose explicit

Six dimensions.
One connected approach.

Each dimension asks a different question. Together, they connect the purpose of the data to their structure, generation, constraints, assessment and use.

The pathway supports iteration: a finding can lead to an earlier decision being reconsidered. Risk and Deployment forms one dimension.

TIME DEFINES THE QUESTION↕
Define the decision

A next-day question,
asked after weigh-in.

The application will help a coach interpret expected body mass at a specified competition-morning check.

The starting mass, official mass, preparation time, recovery interval and athlete context define the information available for that prediction.

Understand the evidence gap

Historical answers
are a starting point.

The questionnaire describes reported weight-management patterns. It does not link three measured masses and their times within one competition.

Following-week regain is a different outcome from next-day regain. Published measurements and explicit judo knowledge provide additional evidence for the new records.

Represent the competition

Three measurements.
A defined time window.

Each synthetic record will represent one hypothetical athlete in one competition. The measurement stages and elapsed hours keep the prediction question precise.

The next-day mass is the outcome. It cannot be used as an input to a prediction made at official weigh-in.

Regain = check mass − official mass
Connect the output to its use

An estimate the coach
can interpret.

The intended output combines next-day mass, regain and uncertainty, with the probability of exceeding the applicable IJF random-check limit at the specified time.

The limit is a regulatory comparison. Predictive validity for athletes requires independent competition measurements; synthetic evaluation describes performance under the generation assumptions.
